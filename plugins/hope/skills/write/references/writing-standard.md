# Plain writing standard

Make the meaning easy to see. Apply these rules with judgment; accuracy,
naturalness, and the intended voice take priority over mechanical compliance.

## Write directly

Lead with the conclusion, decision, or requested result. Use familiar words,
concrete subjects, and active verbs. Include technical detail when it helps the
reader understand or act; keep a technical term when it is more precise. Use
one term for one concept throughout the text instead of varying it for style.

Remove repeated framing, stock phrases, vague qualifiers, and words that add no
meaning. State the intended action directly. Do not invent an opposing view or
end with a second summary of an already clear point.

Give each sentence one main idea. Split a sentence that carries several
instructions or claims, and break up a long noun stack unless it is an
established term.

Keep related sentences in concise paragraphs, each with one job. Use headings
for retrieval and lists for real sets, choices, sequences, or comparisons.
Avoid imposing a template or splitting connected prose into fragments. Use the
target format's native paragraphs; separate Markdown paragraphs with a blank
line. Leave typography to the renderer.

## Write steps

When the reader must act, write numbered steps in order. Give each step one
action, stated as an instruction. Put a condition or warning before the action
it affects, and state the expected result when the reader needs it to continue.

## Preserve meaning

Preserve facts, uncertainty, numbers, citations, and voice. Do not silently
change, omit, resolve, demote, or reorder a material claim or ambiguity without
authority. Keep exact code, commands, identifiers, interface text, quotations,
and legal language unless changing them is part of the request.

Write each language naturally instead of copying another language's word order.
Follow project conventions and explicit user preferences.

## Check once

Read for clarity, consistent terms, naturalness, and preservation of meaning.
Correct material problems introduced by the edit and stop when the requested
result is complete.

The direct-writing principles adapt George Orwell's six rules in
[Politics and the English Language](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/).
The rules for consistent terms, one idea per sentence, and steps adapt
[ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English](https://www.asd-ste100.org/) without
its controlled dictionary or word limits.
