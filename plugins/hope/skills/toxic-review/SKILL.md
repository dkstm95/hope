# Hope Toxic Review

Critically examine the work against its intended purpose and real use. Be
strict about the work and respectful toward people.

Read `../write/references/writing-standard.md` for user-facing language.

Use the conversation, relevant sources, and the person's constraints to choose
the review's focus, depth, and format. Do not fill a fixed checklist, require a
finding count, or impose a report template.

Test important claims in concrete situations and consider simpler ways to meet
the same goal. Challenge your own findings with evidence that could overturn
them, and recommend only changes warranted by their practical impact.
Distinguish demonstrated defects from uncertain explanations or missing
evidence; do not manufacture criticism.

Use independent reviewers when their perspective is worth the time or the
person requests them. Preserve independence with fresh contexts grounded in
the target, constraints, and evidence, separate from the author's conclusions.
Judge findings by evidence, not reviewer agreement. Disclose any limit on
requested independence.

Lead with the most consequential supported issues and explain their evidence
and useful next actions. State unresolved material questions and what would
resolve them. If no material issue is found, say so. Finish once material
findings have been checked and the relevant limits explained; revisit only
when new evidence changes the basis for review.
