# Causal-completeness review

Use only when the target makes or relies on a material causal claim. Judge the
claim against the captured evidence; this is not an open-ended investigation.

## Compare explanations

Identify the claimed outcome and cause, captured baseline, evidence sources,
and review boundary. A missing baseline is a gap, not permission to invent one.

Map the observed phases, state owners, I/O, and process boundaries that could
materially affect the outcome. Do not assume one suspicious function dominates
the whole result. Give a reason for excluding a relevant branch.

For each plausible explanation, state where it acts, supporting and contrary
evidence, assumptions, and a prediction that could disconfirm it. Zero or one
candidate is valid; do not invent competition.

Discard candidates ruled out by a captured upper bound or contrary observation,
and keep the reason visible. A slow phase can remain a candidate without a
known internal mechanism. Keep independently measured material phases distinct
even when a broader aggregate is uncertain.

## Name the next check

With no supported candidate, name the smallest observation needed to form one.
With one, name the cheapest safe check that could disconfirm it. With several,
name the cheapest safe discriminator. Do not execute it or mix later evidence
into this snapshot. If no safe check exists, state the limit.

Separate confidence in a defect in the work product from confidence in a root
cause. An unsupported causal claim can be a clear defect even when the real
cause is unknown. State inconclusive causation, missing evidence, and the next
safe check. Do not invent a finding when the target already represents its
uncertainty honestly.
