# Causal-completeness review

Use this method only when the work product makes or relies on a material causal
claim.

The job is to judge the claim and the evidence behind it.

It is not an unbounded incident investigation.

## Bind the claim

Record:

- the claimed outcome;
- the claimed cause;
- the captured baseline, or the fact that none is available;
- the relevant evidence sources; and
- the boundary of the review.

Treat a missing baseline as an evidence gap.

Do not invent one.

## Map the relevant flow

Map only the end-to-end phases, state owners, I/O, and process boundaries that
can materially affect the claimed outcome.

Include every observed material phase.

Exclude an unrelated branch with a concrete reason.

Do not start from one suspicious local function and assume that it dominates
the end-to-end result.

## Compare supported candidates

A candidate is a still-plausible explanation for a material share of the
outcome at the highest level supported by the evidence.

Zero or one candidate is valid.

Do not invent an alternative merely to create competition.

For each candidate, record:

- the level or boundary where it acts;
- supporting evidence;
- assumptions;
- evidence that weakens it; and
- one prediction that could disconfirm it.

Remove a candidate when a captured upper bound or contrary observation rules
out a material contribution.

Keep that rejection visible without counting it as a live candidate.

An observed long phase may remain a phase-level candidate even when its internal
mechanism is unknown.

Do not merge independently measured material phases merely because a broader
aggregate is also uncertain.

## Choose the next check

- With no supported candidate, name the minimum observation needed to form one.
- With one candidate, name the lowest-cost safe check that could disconfirm it.
- With two or more candidates, name the lowest-cost safe discriminator.

Do not execute the check inside this review.

Do not mix later evidence into the captured snapshot.

When no safe check exists, state that limit and stop.

## Report the result

Separate confidence in a defect in the work product from confidence in a root
cause.

An unsupported causal claim can be a clear review finding even when the real
cause remains unknown.

State when causation is inconclusive.

Name the missing evidence and the next safe check.

Do not manufacture a finding when the work product already represents its
uncertainty honestly.
